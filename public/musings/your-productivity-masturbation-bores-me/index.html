Your Productivity Masturbation Bores Me

September 11, 2026

Like clockwork, around this time every year (right when school starts for those of us in the US/Canada), we see YouTube and social media blow up with people showcasing their “school setups” for maximum producticity. There’s no shortage of all the different softwares, app setups, and other eye candy which they utilitze to showcase how productive they are during the academic year.

It’s all bullshit.

Genuinely, trying to build the perfect productivity setup is a great recipe for never getting anything productive done, because you’ll always be focused on trying to “optimize further”. I’ve written before in a previous blog post about my distain for the San Francisco-style “optimization culture” that is sweeping over Generation Z, but for many people trying to figure out how to get better grades is their first look into this setup.

Now this isn’t to say that I don’t use some of these tools myself. In fact, I’ve written about my love of some of these tools in the past as well as how I personally write notes in college. I love Obsidian and Anki for taking lecture notes as well as preparing flashcards, and God knows that I’d be much less organized as an individual if I didn’t have them. But to act like so many people do online and say that “this is the secret to unlocking productivity” just leads people to go on a massive wild goose chase while getting absolutely nothing done as they try to learn a new app or method.

There’s also the risk of trying to become so productive/optimized in studying that you begin offloading the actual learning or reasoning to Large Language Models. I see this time and time again with people trying to use ChatGPT to help them study. I’ve tried the same thing, and I’m convinced that it doesn’t work for anyone, and if they think it does work for them, they’re either lying or have fooled themselves into thinking they are in fact learning better using LLMs.

At a certain point, you have to figure out what works for you. And that’s different for everyone. Just like none of us share the same DNA, none of us also share the same way of thinking or processing information. Sure, there might be enough similarities in some ways that we can categorize people into different types of thinkers, but there’s no such thing as “the perfect productivity setup”. There’s only your perfect setup.

Nobody cares about this metaphorical dick measuring contest where we try to see who has the slickest “workflows”. What’s the actual point of all of this shit if you’re not actually writing down anything of substance? At that point you might as well buy a copy of “War and Peace” and just accept you’re being a performative intellectual who doesn’t know anything.
