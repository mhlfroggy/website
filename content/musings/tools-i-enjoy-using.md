Being a university student studying both mathematics and computer science, as
well as someone who works full time, the tools I use everyday are vital to my
success in both school and life. This is even more important for me as I have
Bipolar disorder and Autism. Here are some of my favorite tools that I use every
day.

### Nix
While Homebrew is the conventional pick for a package manager on macOS, I would
argue that Nix is the better option. The key reason is *reproducability*, which
means that if I have to wipe my laptop or I get a new machine, I can quickly
bootstrap a new system since I have all my settings/options preconfigured. Nix
also allows you to manage any Homebrew packages you need, which is my preferred
method. The X-Factor of Nix however is that you can specify a "development
shell" which contains packages/utilities that you only need for one program,
which is great for keeping your machine clean and free from package bulk.

### Obsidian
I've been using digital note taking systems for a while (I started with Evernote
and Microsoft OneNote), but once I switched to Obsidian was when note taking
became a major part of my life. I have both Autism and Bipolar Disorder so being
able to quickly make notes and jot down idea is incredibly helpful for me, and
Obsidian's plugin system is bar none the best for note taking. I also wrote
several custom Python scripts, which you can find on my
[GitHub](https://github.com/mhlfroggy/obsidian-help-cli). I highly reccommend
using the [Zettelkasten](https://zettelkasten.de/overview/) for note taking, as
it's beautifully messy but easy to find information through. Just don't let AI
manage your notes, because your notes should be a reflection of your mind, not
what a computer thinks is the "optimal" way to do it.

### Rex
I have been using Ghostty as my preferred terminal for years now (both on macOS
and Linux), so when the creator (Mitchell Hashimoto) announced a new terminal
multiplexer built on top of `libghostty` I signed up immediately for the beta
and have been using it ever since. While I do like the customizability of
Zellij/Tmux, Rex's "batteries included" setup makes it quite enjoyable to use
with multiple panes, such as for coding agents as well as neovim.

### Oh-My-Pi (OMP)
My only real use case for LLMs such as Claude is within programming, however I
didn't like the standard Claude Code utility and felt it was too bulky for what
I wanted. OMP is a great solution to that as it builds off the hyper-minimal Pi
coding agent with a "batteries included" philosophy which integrates perfectly
into my neovim workflow. OMP is also unique in that it has a Rust core as
opposed to TypeScript, which in my opinion makes it more stable/trustable
compared to other agents such as T3 Code.

### Apple Watch SE3 + Bevel Health
I used to think that the Apple Watch was an overrated "tech bro" device, but
after purchasing a used SE3 off of Facebook marketplace it's changed how I
work/interact with technology through the day. I particularly like the health
tracking that it provides, and I paired it with the Bevel Health app to get
WHOOP like health metrics without the annoyance of paying a subscription for a
WHOOP band (seriously? We're still paying $240US a year for a fitness wearable
without GPS?). I do reccomend disabling pretty much all notifications and the
Fitness Circles on the watch, and using it more as a tracker for general trends
in your health.

### Fellow Stagg XF
I enjoy working at cafes as much as the next guy, but coffee prices are insane
now (A pour over at my local cafe is almost $7 before tax) so I think purchasing
a coffee maker for home is a great way to save money. I now brew most of my
coffee at home, and go to cafes maybe twice per week, but now getting coffee at
a cafe is now much more enjoyable.

### Maruman Spiral Dot-Ruled Notebooks + Pentel Orenznero Mechanical Pencil
I used an iPad for about a year for taking notes for my mathematic/physics
courses, but it never felt quite right for me and the ergonomics felt wrong. I
ended up switching back to paper/pencil for note taking, but I reccomend getting
dot ruled paper for mathematics as well as a good quality auto-feeding pencil.
